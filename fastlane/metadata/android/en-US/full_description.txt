foomms is an SMS and MMS messenger that encrypts and signs your messages with PGP. It does no cryptography itself: it asks foodjis, which relies on your security key.

What you can do:

• Write to a number: the message is encrypted for its holder's certificate and signed by your key. Read what you are sent, and know who signed it.
• Write to a group of up to twenty people: everyone sees who wrote and replies to all, and the list of members travels with each message. Or send the same message to each separately, encrypted for them alone.
• Tell a message at a glance, by foodjis's colours: green encrypted and certified, blue signed and certified, yellow encrypted, red in clear and not certified, grey closed.
• Tie a number to its holder's certificate, in your address book.
• Attach a photo, a picture, a contact, where you are or a file; record a voice message by holding the empty field.
• Search the conversations; back them up and restore them in the "SMS Backup & Restore" format.
• Make it the phone's messaging application.

What foomms does better than messengers run by companies:
• end-to-end encryption under your key alone, which nobody else holds;
• interoperability: the person you write to can use another PGP application, nothing locks you into foomms;
• no central server, nothing but your operator's network;
• not even any need for the Internet;
• digital sobriety: a message rarely exceeds 300 KB, photos and videos cut down to what it takes to be seen.

Holding your key to the phone once opens several messages at a time, which close again after the time you choose.

The application asks for no account, goes through no server of ours and shows no advertising: the phone network carries the messages. Provided the operator still carries MMS: some no longer do, or never did, as in Germany or Japan. All that is left there are messengers that go through servers on the Internet, which makes global surveillance easier.

You need foodjis, a contactless OpenPGP security key and a phone with an NFC reader.

Free software, under the GPL-3.0-only licence: the source code is given to anyone who holds the application and asks for it.
